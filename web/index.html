Computational Economy

Households, factories, traders, credit banks, central banks and states trade in three currency zones. Prices, interest rates and money supply emerge from each agent's own decisions. The simulation runs in your browser. Its optimizers for CES and Cobb-Douglas utility and production run in an asm.js module.

Markets

Daily closing prices of settled trades in the selected currency. Factories raise prices when they sell out and lower them when stock remains.

Money and central bank

The central bank sets the key interest rate from the gap between the price index and its target, which grows by the 2 % inflation target per year. Credit banks create deposits by lending.

Households

Each household sells 16 labour hours a day and spends its income on a CES utility bundle that includes leisure. A household that lacks utility for too long leaves the economy, and adults found new households.

National accounts

Balance sheets that all agents published on the last completed day, aggregated by agent type, and money transferred between agent types on that day.

JavaScript port of uwol/computational-economy (GPL v3). The model and its parameters follow the Java version unchanged. The economic models are the original .properties configurations. Population scaling multiplies the numbers of households, factories, credit banks and traders.

Hard cash (M0) is not used by any agent in this model, so money supply starts at M1.
